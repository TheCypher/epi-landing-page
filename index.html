How an EPI meter measures flow

Two pieces of engineering do the work: a matched pair of platinum sensors, and a patented tube that averages flow across the whole pipe.

Thermal mass sensing

Each sensor uses two ratiometrically matched, reference-grade platinum RTDs sheathed in 316 stainless steel, or Hastelloy C when needed. One is heated; the other tracks gas temperature. As gas carries heat away, the power needed to hold the difference rises in proportion to mass flow, so the reading is true mass flow, not volume.

Read the full guide to flow meters

Flow Averaging Tube™

A row of inlet ports along the probe samples flow at several points across the pipe. The pressure from each port is averaged inside the tube, so the sensor sees the true average flow. In many systems the upstream straight run can drop to as little as three pipe diameters, which helps where space is tight.
